## Species-Specific Behavioral Analysis

### 1. Barn Swallow (Hirundo rustica)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Begin roosting 10 minutes after sunset, sleep 55 minutes later[1]
- In cloudy/rainy weather, perch 5 minutes before sunset[1]
- Form large communal roosts near water sources[26]
- Roost sites spaced approximately 100-200km apart during migration[26]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Peak activity during morning and afternoon hours[26]
- Forage primarily within 1km of nesting site[5]
- Aerial insectivore, catching insects on the wing[5]
- Feed continuously during daylight migration[26]

**Active Time:** Dawn to dusk with peak activity in morning and late afternoon

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Open grassland habitat** similar to preferred foraging areas
- **Abundant flying insects** disturbed by airport activities and attracted to lights
- **Thermal currents** from runways ideal for aerial maneuvering
- **Safe roosting structures** on airport buildings and infrastructure[23]

### 2. Black Kite (Milvus migrans)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Form large communal winter roosts[21]
- Show large seasonal fluctuations with highest numbers July-October after monsoons[21]
- Roost in tall trees or suitable elevated structures[24]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Most active during day with earliest arrival at dawn, last departure after dusk[30]
- Opportunistic scavenger, attracted to garbage dumps and organic waste[27]
- Prefer flying low over ground to foraging from ground[30]
- Cleptoparasitic behavior - steal food from other birds[30]

**Active Time:** Dawn to dusk with continuous activity, peak during midday thermals

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Waste management areas** providing abundant scavenging opportunities[27]
- **Thermal updrafts** from runway surfaces ideal for soaring[21]
- **Rodent populations** in airport grasslands[27]
- **Elevated perching sites** on airport infrastructure[24]

### 3. Eurasian Kestrel (Falco tinnunculus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Generally solitary, spending large amounts of time perching within home range[59]
- Prefer elevated perching sites 3-9m height for hunting[56]
- Use buildings, trees, or artificial structures for roosting[40]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Diurnal hunters, most active during daylight hours[59]
- Characteristic hovering behavior 10-20m above ground when hunting[56]
- Hunt primarily small mammals (voles, mice) and insects[56]
- Males increase foraging during breeding season to provision females[43]

**Active Time:** Diurnal, most active during mid-morning to late afternoon

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Short grassland habitat** ideal for detecting small mammal prey[56]
- **Abundant rodent populations** in airport vegetation areas
- **Elevated perching opportunities** on airport structures and lighting[40]
- **Open spaces** perfect for hovering hunting technique[56]

### 4. House Crow (Corvus splendens)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Roost communally near human habitations, often over busy streets[28]
- Prefer well-lit areas with human activity, close to food sources[28]
- Pre-roosting aggregations on buildings, TV antennas before final roost[28]
- Roosting sites coincide with feeding sites[25]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Enhanced foraging activity in early morning and late afternoon[25]
- Opportunistic omnivore, feeds on refuse, small animals, insects[28]
- Highly social feeding, often in groups of 3-30 individuals[25]
- Four feeding methods: searching, stealing, caching, accepting offerings[25]

**Active Time:** Dawn to dusk with peak activity early morning and late evening

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Food waste and garbage** from airport facilities and passenger areas[25]
- **Safe roosting sites** on airport buildings and structures[28]
- **Water sources** for drinking and bathing
- **Human activity areas** providing diverse feeding opportunities[25]

### 5. Rose-ringed Parakeet (Psittacula krameri)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Large communal roosts in tall trees, particularly metallic species[60]
- Prefer roosts less than 1km from croplands and feeding areas[60]
- Trees like Salmalia malabarica, Terminalia arjuna preferred for roosting[60]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Primarily granivorous, 80% of diet seed-based[63]
- Active foraging in early morning and evening[60]
- Feed in flocks, exhibits allo-feeding and allo-preening behaviors[60]
- Seasonal fruit feeding, particularly ripe fruits over unripe[57]

**Active Time:** Dawn and dusk peak activity, midday rest periods

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Seed-bearing grasses** in airport landscaping areas
- **Fruit trees** in airport beautification zones[57]
- **Tall roosting trees** or suitable substitute structures
- **Open feeding areas** with good visibility for flock feeding[60]

### 6. Common Myna (Acridotheres tristis)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Communal roosting throughout year except incubating/brooding females[61]
- Form large groups after March, communal roosts in hundreds[58]
- Split up mornings for foraging, return to roosts in evening[58]
- Regular movement between roost sites and foraging grounds[58]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Prefer open, grassy habitats for foraging[64]
- Active during morning (08:30-12:30) and afternoon (14:30-17:00) sessions[61]
- Feed alone or in groups on insects, fruits, seeds[64]
- Opportunistic feeding behavior[58]

**Active Time:** Bimodal - morning and afternoon peaks, midday rest

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Open grassland areas** ideal for ground foraging[64]
- **Building cavities** for potential nesting sites[64]
- **Insect populations** in maintained grass areas
- **Safe communal roosting sites** on airport infrastructure[58]

### 7. Rock Pigeon (Columba livia)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Non-migratory, strong site fidelity[95]
- Roost on ledges, buildings, bridges - artificial cliff substitutes[92]
- Multiple broods per year (up to 5+), continuous breeding potential[98]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Forage primarily by walking on ground[92]
- Feed in flocks with "producer" and "scrounger" dynamics[98]
- Diet: seeds, grains, human food waste in urban areas[92]
- Active throughout day with peak morning and evening activity

**Active Time:** Diurnal with continuous activity, peak dawn and dusk

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Building ledges and structures** mimicking natural cliff habitat[95]
- **Seed sources** from airport landscaping and maintenance[92]
- **Water sources** for drinking (unique continuous drinking ability)[98]
- **Safe nesting sites** with minimal disturbance from predators[95]

### 8. Red-wattled Lapwing (Vanellus indicus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Ground nesting species preferring open habitats[96]
- Breed March-August with shallow ground scrape nests[96]
- Territorial during breeding season[93]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Opportunistic feeders: insects, mollusks, grains[96]
- Active throughout day with high vigilance behavior[99]
- Spend large proportion of time on vigilance (44%), locomotion (19%), foraging (13%)[99]

**Active Time:** Diurnal with continuous activity, highest vigilance during breeding

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Open grassland areas** perfect for ground nesting[96]
- **Short vegetation** providing good visibility for predator detection[99]
- **Abundant invertebrate prey** in maintained grass areas
- **Minimal ground disturbance** in certain airport zones[93]

### 9. Asian Koel (Eudynamys scolopaceus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Brood parasites, use crow nests for reproduction[97]
- Males not involved in chick care, females occasionally feed young[97]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Primarily frugivorous adults, feeding mainly on fruits[97]
- Omnivorous diet including insects, caterpillars, eggs[97]
- Most active at dawn with distinctive calls[94]
- Defend fruiting trees during foraging[97]

**Active Time:** Most active dawn and early morning, also active at dusk

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Fruiting trees** in airport landscaping[94]
- **Large trees** suitable for perching and calling[97]
- **Crow populations** necessary for brood parasitism[97]
- **Quiet areas** with mature vegetation for fruit foraging[94]

### 10. Yellow-wattled Lapwing (Vanellus malabaricus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Sedentary species with seasonal movements triggered by monsoons[128]
- Ground nesters in open habitats[128]
- Territory size approximately 2.7 acres[128]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Diet consists mainly of beetles, termites, invertebrates[128]
- Ground foraging species[128]
- Sharp "tchee-it" calls during active periods[128]

**Active Time:** Diurnal activity with peak periods morning and evening

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Open ground areas** suitable for territory establishment[128]
- **Beetle and termite populations** in grass root systems
- **Minimal ground disturbance** in designated zones
- **Short grass areas** for effective ground foraging[128]

### 11. Indian Peafowl (Pavo cristatus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Roost on tall trees (8-23m height) at 7-18m above ground[126]
- Prefer stout-branched trees accommodating long train feathers[126]
- Communal roosting at location with undisturbed habitat[129]
- Use buildings/rooftops in residential areas[126]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Spend 39.8% of time feeding, 26.3% locomotion, 13.2% resting[112]
- Morning basking, afternoon shade-seeking behavior[126]
- Feed on wide range including groundnuts, tomato, paddy, chilly[126]
- Forage in agricultural fields, parks, forest outskirts[126]

**Active Time:** Dawn to dusk with midday rest periods

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Large open areas** for foraging and movement[112]
- **Tall roosting structures** or trees within airport boundary[126]
- **Diverse food sources** from airport landscaping[126]
- **Minimal predator pressure** in secure airport environment[131]

### 12. Eastern Cattle Egret (Bubulcus ibis)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Communal roosting in thousands[127]
- Form pre-roost gatherings to loaf, preen, drink before flying to roost trees[127]
- Travel from foraging grounds to night roost sites in late afternoon[127]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Specialist in commensal feeding with large mammals[127]
- Most active morning and afternoon, loaf during midday when cattle rest[127]
- Walking Slowly feeding behavior adjacent to animals[127]
- Highly social feeding in flocks of dozens to thousands[127]

**Active Time:** Morning and afternoon peaks, midday rest periods

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Grassland areas** suitable for insect hunting[127]
- **Airport vehicles and machinery** as substitute "beaters" for insects[127]
- **Water sources** for drinking and pre-roost gathering[127]
- **Tall trees or structures** for communal roosting[127]

### 13. Indian Roller (Coracias benghalensis)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Generally solitary or in pairs[150]
- Roost on electric/telegraph wires, trees at 3-9m height[150]
- Preening behavior followed by flying around roosting site[150]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Perch hunting from elevated positions[150]
- Feed 45% beetles, 25% grasshoppers and crickets[150]
- Associate with other species that flush prey[150]
- Active during day, may feed late after dusk near lights[150]

**Active Time:** Diurnal with possible late evening activity near artificial lights

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Electric infrastructure** providing ideal perching sites[150]
- **Open grassland** with abundant beetle and grasshopper populations[150]
- **Airport lighting** attracting insects for late evening feeding[150]
- **Minimal disturbance** from predators in airport environment[153]

### 14. Red-vented Bulbul (Pycnonotus cafer)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Communal roosting in dense trees and bushes[151]
- Flocks of 6-70 individuals, minimum in breeding season (2-8), maximum in winter (18-72)[151]
- Return to roost 20 minutes to 1.5 hours before sunset[151]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Omnivorous: fruits, flower petals, nectar, insects[157]
- Highly territorial during breeding season[154]
- Feed in pairs or small groups[157]

**Active Time:** Dawn to dusk with communal roosting behavior

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Dense vegetation** for roosting and nesting[151]
- **Diverse food sources** from airport landscaping[157]
- **Fruit-bearing plants** in beautification areas
- **Safe territory establishment** in less disturbed zones[154]

### 15. Black Drongo (Dicrurus macrocercus)

**Roosting Behavior:**

- Prefer tall trees during breeding season for nest safety[152]
- Use electric power lines during non-breeding season[158]
- Perch lower during breeding to guard nests[158]

**Foraging Patterns:**

- Aerial insectivore using multiple strategies[155]
- 72.5% insect prey using aerial and ground feeding modes[158]
- Most active early morning and evening[158]
- Solitary feeding despite social perching[158]

**Active Time:** Dawn and dusk peaks with continuous diurnal activity

**Airport Attraction Factors:**

- **Electric infrastructure** ideal for perching and prey detection[158]
- **Rich insect populations** in maintained grassland areas[155]
- **Open areas** suitable for aerial hunting techniques[155]
- **Elevated nesting sites** on airport structures during breeding[152]

## Risk Assessment and Recommendations

### High-Risk Species (Immediate Attention Required)

1. **Black Kite** - Large soaring raptors with thermal dependence
2. **Indian Peafowl** - Large ground birds in open areas
3. **House Crow** - Intelligent, adaptable, large flocks
4. **Rock Pigeon** - Continuous presence, building nesters

### Moderate-Risk Species (Regular Monitoring)

1. **Barn Swallow** - Aerial insectivores during migration periods
2. **Common Myna** - Large communal roosts and ground foraging
3. **Eurasian Kestrel** - Hovering behavior over runways
4. **Rose-ringed Parakeet** - Large flocks, loud calls

### Management Strategies

#### Habitat Modification

- **Grass management**: Maintain appropriate height to reduce attractiveness[193]
- **Water source control**: Cover or drain unnecessary water bodies[193]
- **Vegetation selection**: Avoid fruiting/seed-bearing plants near operational areas[192]
- **Waste management**: Strict control of food waste and garbage[25][192]

#### Active Deterrents

- **Thermal disruption**: Methods to reduce runway thermal attraction[195]
- **Perching deterrents**: Modify electrical infrastructure to prevent roosting[158]
- **Sound deterrents**: Species-specific alarm calls during peak activity[151]
- **Visual deterrents**: Reflective devices and predator models[193]

#### Monitoring and Assessment

- **Continuous bird activity monitoring** during peak hours (dawn/dusk)[26][158]
- **Seasonal migration tracking** for species like Barn Swallow[26]
- **Breeding season intensification** of management for territorial species[154]
- **Weather-related activity prediction** for thermal-dependent species[195]

## Conclusion

The bird species identified around CBE Civil Airport represent a complex ecosystem attracted by multiple interconnected factors including food availability, roosting opportunities, thermal currents, and reduced predator pressure. Effective management requires a comprehensive approach addressing habitat modification, active deterrence, and continuous monitoring, with particular attention to high-risk species and peak activity periods.

The success of bird hazard management depends on understanding species-specific behaviors and implementing targeted strategies that address the root causes of attraction rather than relying solely on dispersal methods. Regular assessment and adaptive management approaches will be crucial for maintaining aviation safety while respecting wildlife conservation principles.
